:::scene{type=idea icon=bulb}
text: Architecture now has three kinds of AI. / They do different things.
narration: |
  Architecture now has three kinds of A I at its disposal, and they do three different
  things. Keeping them apart is the first skill of this course, because a tool that
  proposes, a tool that acts, and a tool that predicts have to be checked in three
  different ways.
:::

:::scene{type=cards}
heading: Three kinds of AI, three jobs
items:
  - title: Generative AI
    text: Proposes. Images, text, code, geometry — new possibilities, made from patterns in data.
    tag: proposes
  - title: Agentic AI
    text: Acts. Agents that plan, call tools, negotiate with each other — with you in the loop.
    tag: acts
  - title: Machine & deep learning
    text: Predicts. Models fitted to measured data that estimate how a building will perform.
    tag: predicts
narration: |
  Generative A I proposes. Give it a prompt and it produces an image, a text, a piece of
  code, a shape: a new possibility made from patterns it has seen. Agentic A I acts. An
  agent plans, calls tools, and can negotiate with other agents, with you in the loop.
  And machine learning and deep learning predict. They learn from measured data how a
  building will perform, before you build it. Three kinds, three jobs.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=warn}
text: A plausible output is not evidence.
sub: An image is not a building. A fluent answer is not a fact. A high score on the training data is not a result.
narration: |
  Here is the thread that runs through every module. A plausible output is not
  evidence. An image is not a building. A fluent answer is not a fact. A high score on
  the data a model was trained on is not a result. Each module teaches the checks that
  turn a possibility into a claim you can defend in front of a client, a reviewer, or a
  building inspector.
:::

narration: |
  Eight modules. Module one teaches Python from zero, in the browser, with nothing to
  install. Module two is generative A I: images, prompts, and language models, and the
  judgment to tell a plausible picture from evidence. Module three is coding with an A I
  assistant, and reading what it writes. Module four builds agents that negotiate a
  design with each other. Then the course turns to data. Module five is looking at data
  before you model it. Module six fits machine learning models and evaluates them on
  data they have never seen. Module seven opens the neural network. Module eight applies
  deep networks to images, graphs, time, and physics.
:::

narration: |
  Think of it as two halves. Modules one to four are about making: writing code,
  generating possibilities, coding with an assistant, building agents that act.
  Modules five to eight are about learning from data: looking at it, fitting models to
  it, and understanding the networks behind those models. The two halves meet in the
  hands-on lessons, where the agents you build in module four argue about the energy loads
  that the models of modules six to eight predict.
:::

:::scene{type=cards}

  - title: Generate and check

  - title: Build and orchestrate agents

  - title: Model and evaluate

narration: |
  By the end you will be able to read and write the Python this course uses. You will
  be able to control a generative model and translate its output into something you can
  actually analyse. You will build agents and run a design negotiation on your own laptop.
  And you will fit models to building data, evaluate them on unseen data, and say clearly
  what a result does and does not establish.
:::
