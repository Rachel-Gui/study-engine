:::scene{type=title}
narration: |
  Welcome to lesson two point four, control the generator. Beyond the words of the
  prompt, a generator has settings: the seed, the guidance, the negative prompt, the
  number of steps. Each one is a knob. This lesson runs five controlled experiments, one
  knob at a time, and then places all of them on a ladder of control that goes from
  words at the bottom to checkable constraints at the top.
:::

:::scene{type=cards}
heading: The knobs
items:
  - title: Seed
    text: Where the noise starts. Same prompt, four seeds, four different buildings.
    tag: randomness
  - title: Guidance
    text: How strongly the model follows the prompt. 2 is loose, 15 is insistent — and can distort.
    tag: strength
  - title: Negative prompt
    text: '"blurry, distorted, people, text" — what to steer away from. Steering, not enforcing.'
    tag: avoid
  - title: Steps
    text: How many denoising updates. 10, 25, 50, 75 — more time, shrinking gains.
    tag: effort
narration: |
  Four knobs. The seed sets where the noise starts: same prompt, four seeds, four
  different buildings. Guidance sets how strongly the model follows the prompt: two is
  loose, fifteen is insistent, and insistent can distort. The negative prompt lists
  what to steer away from, blurry, distorted, people, text, and it is steering, not
  enforcing. And steps is how many denoising updates you pay for: ten, twenty five,
  fifty, seventy five, with more time and shrinking gains.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=warn}
text: Compare settings within an experiment, / never across them.
sub: The seed study, the guidance study and the steps study use different prompts. A difference between two studies could be the prompt, not the setting.
narration: |
  A rule for reading the lab. Compare settings within one experiment, never across
  experiments. The seed study, the guidance study and the steps study each use a
  different building and a different prompt. So a difference between two studies could
  be the prompt, not the setting you are interested in. Within a study, one thing
  changed. Across studies, everything did.
:::

:::scene{type=code}
heading: One experiment, as the course notebook ran it
code: |
  prompt3 = ("A desert cultural center with rammed earth walls, "
             "shaded courtyard, low horizontal massing, ...")
  for g in [2.0, 5.0, 7.5, 10.0, 15.0]:
      generator = torch.Generator("cuda").manual_seed(123)
      img = pipe(prompt3, num_inference_steps=30,
                 guidance_scale=g, generator=generator).images[0]
output: |
  five images · guidance 2, 5, 7.5, 10, 15 · same prompt, same seed
note: The guidance study from the course notebook, prompt shortened. The seed, negative-prompt, steps and resolution studies are the same loop with a different line changing.
narration: |
  Here is one of the five experiments as the course notebook actually ran it. The
  prompt is fixed: a desert cultural centre with rammed earth walls. A loop runs over
  five guidance values, from two to fifteen. Before every image the generator is reset
  to the same seed, one hundred and twenty three. Steps stay at thirty. So guidance is
  the only thing that changes. The other four experiments are the same loop with a
  different line changing. That is all a controlled experiment is, in code.
:::

:::scene{type=diagram id=control_hierarchy}
heading: The ladder of control
narration: |
  Now step back. Everything in the lab lives on the first two rungs of a ladder.
  Prompt control: words, which steer and guarantee nothing. Settings and stochastic
  control: seed, guidance, steps, negative prompt, which give you repeatable runs and
  stronger adherence, but still no geometry. Above them sit three more rungs. Reference
  conditioning: give the model a picture to follow, a sketch, a depth map, instead of a
  phrase to imagine. Structural control: impose where things go, with edges, masks and
  layouts through a control network. And at the top, explicit design constraints:
  dimensions, adjacencies, code, physics. These require checks
  against a structured model, outside the generator.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=bulb}
text: More control means more work / and more that can be verified.
sub: A prompt is the right rung for exploring. A constraint is the right rung for deciding. Know which rung you are on.
narration: |
  Each rung imposes more and suggests less. More control means more work, and more that
  can be verified. This is not a ranking of good and bad tools. A prompt is the right
  rung for exploring: fast, loose, many options. A constraint is the right rung for
  deciding: slow, exact, one option you can defend. The mistake is not being on the
  wrong rung. The mistake is thinking you are on a higher rung than you are.
:::
