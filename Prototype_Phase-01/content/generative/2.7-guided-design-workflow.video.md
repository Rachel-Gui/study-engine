:::scene{type=title}
narration: |
  Welcome to lesson two point seven, the guided generative design workflow. Everything
  in this module so far has been a separate skill: prompting, controlling, comparing,
  critiquing, translating. This lesson puts them together in one experiment that you
  carry from a design intention to an evaluation, and keep as one record. It is the
  activity that makes the module yours.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=question}
text: What can a generated possibility contribute to your design — / and what still needs your judgment?
narration: |
  The central question, one more time, because now you answer it with your own
  experiment rather than with an example. What can a generated possibility contribute
  to your design, and what still needs your judgment? By the end of the activity you
  will have a specific answer, with evidence.
:::

:::scene{type=steps}
heading: Eight stages, one page
steps:
  - Define — what are you exploring, for whom?
  - Prompt — read the exact fixed prompt
  - Generate — the initial pavilion
  - Control — change only the material
  - Compare — previous against current
  - Critique — one success, one limitation
  - Translate — what is missing before evaluation?
  - "*Reflect — what did generation contribute?"
narration: |
  Eight stages, all on one page of the website. Define: what are you exploring, and
  for whom, and what would make a candidate useful? Prompt: read the exact fixed prompt.
  Generate: select the initial pavilion. Control: change only the material, timber to
  concrete first. Compare: previous against current, with visible evidence. Critique:
  one architectural success and at least one limitation that needs checking. Translate:
  what structured information is missing before evaluation? And reflect: what did
  generation contribute, and where was your judgment essential?
:::

:::scene{type=cards}
heading: Three saved states
items:
  - title: ORIGINAL
    text: The starting output. It never changes.
    tag: fixed
  - title: PREVIOUS
    text: The test just before your latest one — not a permanent baseline.
    tag: moves
  - title: CURRENT
    text: Your latest successful test. The comparison is always PREVIOUS against CURRENT.
    tag: moves
narration: |
  The page keeps three saved states, and the names matter. Original is the starting
  output; it never changes. Previous is the test just before your latest one, not a
  permanent baseline. Current is your latest successful test. Every comparison is
  previous against current, and editing a control without testing changes nothing.
  The final record compares original with current, and keeps the settings in between.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=warn}
text: Predict before you test. / Write before you reveal.
sub: The record is only evidence if your expectation was written down before you saw the result.
narration: |
  One rule turns this from a picture gallery into an experiment. Predict before you
  test, and write before you reveal. What do you expect the one changed variable to
  affect? Write it. Then test, then look, then write what actually changed and what
  changed unexpectedly. The record is only evidence if your expectation was written
  down before you saw the result. Afterwards, everyone is a prophet.
:::

:::scene{type=quiz}
question: You changed only the material, timber to concrete, and the roof shape changed too. What does that show?
options:
  - That concrete roofs are always curved
  - That one prompt term can change several visual qualities — terms are not independent variables
  - That the seed must have changed as well
answer: 1
pause: 5
explain: The seed and settings were held fixed. The model brought the whole learned look of concrete along with the word. That is an observation about this pair, not a mechanism for every difference — repeat across seeds before you generalise.
narration: |
  A check. You changed only the material, timber to concrete, and the roof shape changed
  too. What does that show? A: that concrete roofs are always curved. B: that one
  prompt term can change several visual qualities, so terms are not independent
  variables. C: that the seed must have changed as well. Pause and pick.
after: |
  It is B. The seed and every setting were held fixed; the model brought the whole
  learned look of concrete along with the word. Be careful with the size of that claim.
  It is an observation about this pair, not a mechanism for every difference. Repeat
  across several seeds before you say anything general.
:::

:::scene{type=list}
heading: What a useful record contains
items:
  - The design intention — and the exact input you changed
  - Your expectation — written before the test
  - Visible evidence — what changed, and what changed unexpectedly
  - The original and current prompts, with their images
  - What is missing, who could verify it, and the next step
narration: |
  What makes a record useful to someone who was not in the room? The design intention
  and the exact input you changed. Your expectation, written before the test. Visible
  evidence: what changed and what changed unexpectedly. The original and current
  prompts, with their images. And what is missing, who or what could verify it, and a
  concrete next step. There is no single correct image. The reasoning is what makes the
  experiment reviewable.
:::
