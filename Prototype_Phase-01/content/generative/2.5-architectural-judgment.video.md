:::scene{type=idea icon=eye}
text: Separate what you see, / what you infer, / and what you cannot know.
sub: An observation, an inference, and an unknown are three different kinds of statement. A critique that keeps them apart is one you can act on.
narration: |
  The whole lesson is one habit. When you look at a generated image, separate three
  things. What you see: there is a projecting upper volume. What you infer: it is
  probably a reading room. And what you cannot know from this view: whether anything
  holds it up. An observation, an inference and an unknown are three different kinds of
  statement. A critique that keeps them apart is one you can act on.
:::

  openings change size and spacing, and the image says nothing about the rooms behind
  them. The stair: it dominates the approach, and the step-free route is nowhere to be
  seen. The lower entrance, at the right: how does it connect to the levels above? And
  the levels themselves: the sloping site makes floor counting ambiguous. One more
  question has no box at all: performance. Soft daylight and timber cladding suggest an
  atmosphere, and establish nothing about energy or materials.

  ambiguous. Then: it is likely that the cantilever is structurally supported, as it is
  a common design feature for buildings of this type. Read that reason again. It is not
  evidence from the image. It is what is typical. A confident caption is a hypothesis to
  check against drawings, not a measurement.

:::scene{type=compare}
heading: Plausible or valid?
left:
  title: Visually plausible
  points:
    - Coherent light and shadow
    - Materials that look right
    - A composition you would photograph
    - Learned from thousands of real photos
right:
  title: Architecturally valid
  points:
    - A load path that works
    - An accessible route that connects
    - Levels that align in section
    - Performance you can simulate
verdict: Plausibility is what the model was trained for. Validity is what you are trained for.
narration: |
  The distinction the whole module turns on. Visually plausible: coherent light and
  shadow, materials that look right, a composition you would photograph. The model was
  trained on thousands of real photographs, so plausibility is exactly what it is good
  at. Architecturally valid: a load path that works, an accessible route that connects,
  levels that align in section, performance you can simulate. Plausibility is what the
  model was trained for. Validity is what you are trained for.
:::
