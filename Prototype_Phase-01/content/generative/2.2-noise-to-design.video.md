:::scene{type=title}
narration: |
  Welcome to lesson two point two, from noise to design. Most A I images today come
  from diffusion models. In this short lesson you will see what diffusion actually does,
  why an image appears out of what looks like television static, and which three
  settings control the process. Then you will move a slider on the website and watch it
  happen.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=spark}
text: Diffusion starts from noise / and removes it, step by step.
sub: A text encoder turns your prompt into a condition. A denoising model takes the noisy image and makes it a little less noisy. Thirty or fifty times.
narration: |
  Here is the idea in one line. Diffusion starts from pure noise and removes it, step
  by step. A text encoder turns your prompt into a condition. A denoising model takes
  the noisy image and makes it a little less noisy, in a way that fits the condition.
  Then it does that again. Thirty times, fifty times. Structure appears first, then
  detail. The model does not draw the picture. It uncovers one.
:::

:::scene{type=steps}
heading: One generation, step by step
steps:
  - Noise — random pixels
  - Text condition — your prompt, encoded
  - Denoise — a little less noise, guided by the prompt
  - Repeat — 30 to 50 steps
  - "*An image — one sample from the distribution"
narration: |
  Step one, noise: random pixels, nothing there. Step two, the text condition: your
  prompt, turned into numbers the model can use. Step three, denoise: the model
  predicts what noise to remove, guided by the condition, and removes a little of it.
  Step four, repeat, thirty to fifty times. And out comes an image: one sample from
  everything the model learned that fits your words. Many pipelines do this in a
  compressed space rather than on the pixels you see, but the loop is the same.
:::

:::scene{type=cards}
heading: Three things control an iterative process
items:
  - title: Condition
    text: What are we asking for? The prompt — a library, its setting, materials, light.
    tag: what
  - title: Seed
    text: Where does the noise start? A different seed gives a different image from the same prompt.
    tag: where from
  - title: Inference steps
    text: How many denoising updates? More costs time; the gains shrink and depend on the pipeline.
    tag: how many
narration: |
  Three things control the process. The condition: what are we asking for? The prompt
  describes a library, its setting, materials, light. The seed: where does the noise
  start? A different seed gives a different image from the very same prompt, because
  the starting noise was different. And the number of inference steps: how many
  denoising updates? More steps cost more time, and the gains shrink. Ten steps is
  rough, thirty is usually enough, seventy five is mostly waiting.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=warn}
text: A seed is not a design identifier.
sub: The same seed gives the same image only when the model, the version, the scheduler and every setting are also the same. Record all of them.
narration: |
  One thing beginners get wrong about seeds. A seed is not a design identifier. The same
  seed gives the same image only when the model, its version, the scheduler and every
  other setting are also the same. Change the software and the same seed gives a
  different picture. If you want to reproduce a result, record everything: model,
  version, prompt, seed, guidance, steps. Lesson two point six shows you how to keep
  that record in code.
:::

  - How much detail a real model resolves at each numbered step

  A check before you use the playground. The diffusion playground on the website has a
  slider from noise to image. What does it actually show you? A: the exact intermediate
  images a real model produced. B: the idea of structure emerging step by step, a
  teaching simulation. C: how much detail a real model resolves at each step. Pause and

  It is B. The playground fades a fixed image out of noise to illustrate the idea of
  iteration. A real diffusion model applies learned updates, usually in a compressed
  space, and its intermediate states look different. The important idea survives: an
  iterative process, steered by a condition, not a single act of drawing and not a
  lookup of a finished picture.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=eye}
text: A performance hint in a prompt is a hypothesis.
sub: '"Passive cooling" may change what the image looks like. It does not supply airflow rates, wall constructions, or a thermal model.'
narration: |
  Last idea. If you write passive cooling in a prompt, the image may change: deeper
  overhangs, more shade, an open courtyard. That is the model reproducing what passive
  cooling tends to look like in its training images. It supplies no airflow rates, no
  wall constructions, no thermal model. Treat cues like that as hypotheses to check
  later, not as results.
:::

:::scene{type=recap}
items:
  - Diffusion starts from noise and removes it, step by step, guided by the prompt
  - Three controls — condition, seed, inference steps

  To remember. Diffusion starts from noise and removes it step by step, guided by the
