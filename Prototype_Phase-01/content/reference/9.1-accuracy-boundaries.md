## Accuracy boundaries

Every script, diagram, and narration in this course is checked against this list
before it ships. If you are writing a new episode, read it before you start, not
after.

:::narration
Before you write anything for this course, read this list. It is not a compliance
checkbox. Every item on it is a mistake that is easy to make, common in published
work, and hard to walk back once a student has learned it. The last one is the one
people skip most: always state the range. A number with no stated range is not a
result.
:::

- Do not describe neural networks as systems that literally think like a human brain.
- Do not interpret correlation as causation.
- Do not interpret a p-value as the probability that a hypothesis is true, or as
  evidence of practical importance.
- Do not interpret high R² or accuracy as sufficient evidence of reliability.
- Do not present deeper networks as automatically more accurate.
- Do not use {{test set}} data for hyperparameter tuning.
- Do not allow records from the same building or simulation family to leak across
  training and test sets.
- Do not describe a graph, hypergraph, adjacency algorithm, or self-organizing map as
  a {{GNN}} unless a neural message-passing model is used.
- Do not use physics-informed ML, {{PINN}}, hybrid model, {{surrogate model}}, and
  {{INR}} as interchangeable terms.
- Do not imply that a fast surrogate always replaces high-fidelity simulation.
- Do not treat a realistic visual output as evidence of constructability or
  performance.
- **Always state** the data range, geometry range, climate range, metrics,
  {{uncertainty}}, and known failure conditions.

:::keyidea
Before public production, verify each external figure, numerical claim, DOI,
publication status, and license. Prefer redrawing explanatory diagrams in the team's
own visual language.
:::

## Where the course keeps its own promises

The two rules that are easiest to state and hardest to keep are demonstrated rather
than asserted:

:::compare
"High R² is not reliability" | Episode 3.7 | The same model scores R² 0.380 on a random split of ten-minute data and collapses on a chronological one. Same data, same network, honest number is the lower one.
"Deeper is not better" | Episode 3.1 | Measured on that dataset: 64-32 reached R² 0.380, while 512-256-128-64 reached 0.353. Capacity is not accuracy.
:::
