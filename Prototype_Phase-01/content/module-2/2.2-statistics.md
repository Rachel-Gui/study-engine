## Central question

Why does a model that fits the data well still tell you very little on its own?

:::narration
Every model output is a claim about the world, and statistics is the discipline of
knowing how much a claim is worth. Three ideas do most of the work in this course.
Variation: a single number without a spread is not a result. Sampling: the data you
have is a sample, and the question is always what it is a sample of. And
uncertainty: a prediction with no stated range cannot be acted on responsibly.
:::

## Three ideas that carry the course

:::cards
Variation | A number without a spread is not a result | Report the range, not just the mean
Sampling | Your data is a sample of something | Ask what, and what it excludes
Uncertainty | Every prediction has a range | State it, or the number cannot be acted on
:::

## Correlation is not causation

Two quantities can move together because one drives the other, because a third drives
both, or because of how the sample was drawn. A model that predicts well is exploiting
association. It has not established mechanism.

:::keyidea
A coefficient tells you how the model's prediction changes with a feature *while the
other included features are held constant*. Change the feature set and every
coefficient changes. That is a property of the fit, not of the building.
:::

## What a p-value is not

:::compare
What it is | The probability of data this extreme if nothing were going on | A statement about the data under an assumption
What it is not | The probability that your hypothesis is true | And it is not a measure of practical importance either
:::

:::keyidea
A statistically significant effect can be far too small to matter for a design
decision. Report the effect size and its range, not just whether a threshold was
crossed.
:::
