:::scene{type=code}
heading: An advocate is a few lines of English
code: |
  Agent(
      name="Energy Efficiency Advocate",
      role="Argue for minimal operational energy use",
      instructions="""You are the energy efficiency advocate.
  Your ONLY concern is reducing the building's operational energy.
  Argue from these angles:
  - Is the surface-to-volume ratio efficient for this climate?
  - Is the WWR too high, causing heat loss or cooling load?
  Propose a specific lower WWR and say why.""",
      color="#4b2e83", icon="bolt")
note: Scope it, give a checklist, force a commitment. The same four moves from Lesson 4.1.
narration: |
  Here is an advocate. A name. A role: argue for minimal operational energy use. And
  the instructions, in plain English: you are the energy efficiency advocate, your only
  concern is reducing the building's operational energy, argue from these angles, and
  propose a specific lower window to wall ratio and say why. Scope it, give a
  checklist, force a commitment. Then a colour and an icon for the dashboard. That is
  an agent.
:::

:::scene{type=idea icon=warn}
text: Write the second advocate / to conflict with the first.
sub: If both want the same thing, the manager has nothing to arbitrate, and you have built a very slow summariser.
narration: |
  The one design rule of the negotiation. Write the second advocate to conflict with the
  first. If the energy advocate wants less glass, write a daylight advocate that wants
  more, with its own angles and its own number. If both want the same thing, the
  manager has nothing to arbitrate, and you have built a very slow summariser.
:::

:::scene{type=list}
heading: Run, read, iterate
items:
  - Save the file — the header shows your agent count, "4 agents online"
  - Click Debate — quick or full; saving alone starts nothing
  - Read each advocate — does it cite the numbers it was given?
  - Read the manager — did it name the conflict, decide, and say what was lost?
  - Sharpen the weak prompt — and run it again
narration: |
  Then the loop. Save the file, and the dashboard header shows your agent count. Click
  debate, quick or full; saving alone starts nothing. Read each advocate: does it cite
  the numbers it was given, or does it ramble? Read the manager: did it name the
  conflict, decide, and say what was lost? Then sharpen the weakest prompt and run it
  again. Three rounds of this teaches more than an hour of reading about agents.
:::

:::scene{type=compare}
heading: What the negotiation establishes, and what it does not

  What does the negotiation establish? A structured set of design tensions, made explicit.
  A named trade off, with what it sacrificed. And a numeric constraint packet the
  sliders can apply. What does it not establish? Any energy, daylight or carbon
  quantity. Any uncertainty on any number. Code compliance, or a licensed
  professional's judgment. The debate surfaces trade offs. It does not measure
  anything.
:::
