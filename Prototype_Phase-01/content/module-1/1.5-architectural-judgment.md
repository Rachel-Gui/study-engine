## Architectural AI Critic

It looks convincing. But what can the image actually establish?

Begin with what the image makes visible, then distinguish an observation from an inference and from something that cannot be established from this view. The image below is a fictional AI-generated teaching example, not a proposed real building or evidence of structural safety.

title: Architectural AI Critic
image: assets/generative/critic-library.png
alt: Monochrome AI-generated library with a long projecting upper volume, irregularly spaced windows, large stairs and a lower entrance.

question: What is attractive about this proposal, and which visible clue makes you want to investigate further?
disclosure: "Inspect the image, record a judgment, and select issues before revealing the review. An image cannot prove that a hidden system is absent."
checks:
  - label: Cantilever and structural load path
    reason: "The upper volume projects beyond the glazed ground floor. Its support is not apparent from this view. Request sections, spans, structural strategy and analysis; do not conclude that a cantilever is impossible merely from the image."
  - label: Stairs, entrance and accessible route
    reason: "A broad stair dominates the approach while a lower entrance appears at the right. The continuous accessible route and connections between levels are unclear. Check plans, levels, circulation and an access strategy."
  - label: Window rhythm and façade logic
    reason: "Openings change size and spacing across the upper façade. This may be intentional, but the image does not establish the relationship to rooms, mullions, assemblies or daylight goals."
  - label: Spatial consistency and floors
    reason: "Several levels are suggested, but the sloping site and partial views make floor counting ambiguous. Establish levels and spatial connections in sections rather than treating visible bands as a measurement."
  - label: Performance and context
    reason: "Soft daylight, timber-like cladding and trees suggest an atmosphere. They do not establish energy demand, material properties, orientation, emissions or whether the image's site context is accurate."
explanation: "Visual plausibility ≠ architectural validity. Use the image to ask better questions, then verify them with structured information and domain judgment."
takeaway: A convincing image still requires architectural judgment; visible cues are not verified structure, circulation or performance.
:::

:::narration
Start with one thing the image does well. Then locate a question that the image cannot settle. The cantilever may be feasible, but we cannot see its load path. The stairs may connect to an accessible route, but the route is not documented here. A useful critic separates what is visible from what has been inferred. Verification follows from those questions, rather than from declaring an attractive image either correct or useless.
:::

:::reflect{feedback="The view does not document loads, spans or support conditions."}

:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
The image provides a clue about a projection, not evidence of member sizes, materials, support conditions or loads. A useful critique specifies what information and analysis would resolve the uncertainty.
:::

## A confident caption is not a measurement

Multimodal models can generate captions or answer questions about architectural images. Compare their responses with visible evidence, and distinguish a plausible description from measured geometric information.

**Illustrative response, not an actual model result:** “This building has three floors.”

:::reflect{feedback="A fluent description does not resolve hidden levels or ambiguous façade bands."}
Can a confident caption establish the number of floors in the library image?
- Use its count as a measured quantity because it considers the whole image
- *Compare visible cues and request plans or sections to resolve ambiguity
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
The sloping site, obscured levels and façade bands admit several interpretations. A model's fluency or confidence is not a calibrated measurement. Use captioning to suggest inspection questions; verify architectural quantities against reliable documentation.
:::

:::keyidea
Generated images offer limited geometric control and do not directly evaluate performance. They may reflect training-data biases or depict non-buildable proposals. Architectural judgment and verification make these possibilities useful.
:::

A material-like texture is a visual treatment, not a specified assembly.
