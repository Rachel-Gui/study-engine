## Central Question

How can generative AI help architects explore design possibilities while maintaining meaningful control and architectural judgment?

:::narration
A convincing architectural image is a starting point for inquiry. It can suggest a material, a spatial atmosphere, or an unexpected relationship to a site. But it does not tell us whether the structure works or how much energy the building needs. This module follows an experiment from its first condition through generation, control, comparison, critique, and translation. The aim is to make possibilities useful without confusing appearance with evidence.
:::

**Learning Objectives**

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

- Distinguish predictive AI from generative AI, and explain diffusion conceptually.
- Construct a structured architectural prompt and explain why one changed term can affect several visual qualities.
- Experiment conceptually with seed, guidance, inference steps, and negative prompts.
- Compare generations systematically and identify architectural limitations.
- Distinguish visual plausibility from architectural validity.
- Explain which structured information is needed before simulation or evaluation.
- Identify where generative AI supports exploration and where human/domain judgment remains necessary.

**Why This Matters**

Prompting expresses design intent, constraints, style and performance hints in language. Generative AI can produce possibilities rapidly, but a convincing image is not the same as a valid architectural decision. Learn to control, compare, critique and translate outputs into architectural workflows.

:::flow
PROMPT / CONDITION > GENERATE > CONTROL > COMPARE > EVALUATE > TRANSLATE
:::

Throughout the arc, **predict → interact → observe → explain → reflect**. The final activity brings your decisions together in one design experiment record.

:::glossarynote
:::

## What does generative mean?

A predictive model maps input characteristics, X, to an estimated outcome, ŷ. A generative model uses a condition to produce a possible new artifact, such as an image or layout.

:::reflect{feedback="Regression estimates an outcome from inputs; it does not itself redesign the building."}
Given a building's window-to-wall ratio, insulation and orientation, what could a regression model estimate?
- *Energy use intensity (EUI) for the described building
- A revised façade design chosen to achieve a target EUI
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
The characteristics form X; predicted EUI is ŷ, a numerical estimate. To judge that prediction, we need measured outcomes and an appropriate evaluation, not just a plausible number.
:::

:::cards
Predictive AI | X → ŷ | Estimate an outcome for a described building.
Generative AI | Condition + randomness → new sample | Produce a possible artifact consistent with learned patterns.
:::

:::reflect{feedback="A condition steers a distribution of possibilities rather than specifying one uniquely correct design."}
Given “a timber community library in Seattle,” what might a text-to-image generative model produce?
- The single design that best satisfies the brief
- *One possible image among many alternatives consistent with the condition
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
The condition steers sampling from learned patterns. It does not specify one uniquely correct image, and the output is not a simulation result. Text, images, layouts and geometry are different possible output representations; an image alone is not reliable geometry.
:::

A {{generative model}} learns patterns in data and samples new outputs resembling those patterns. Think of a {{distribution}} of possibilities rather than a database returning one exact design. A predictive model estimates an answer; a generative model produces plausible possibilities. These are teaching roles rather than mutually exclusive technologies: an LLM, for example, generates text through repeated next-token predictions.

## Model Families Explorer

Open each model family to compare its basic idea, typical outputs and possible architectural roles.

:::details{summary="GAN · generator and discriminator"}
**Idea:** during training, a generator makes samples while a discriminator distinguishes real examples from generated ones. The discriminator is not a structural engineer or an architectural quality assessor.
**Typical output:** images or plan-like layouts.
**Architectural application:** House-GAN / House-GAN++ generate floor-plan layouts conditioned on room adjacency. Similar-looking plans still require circulation and dimensional checks.
:::

:::details{summary="VAE · explore a latent space"}
**Idea:** encode examples into a compressed probabilistic latent space, then decode samples and variations.
**Typical output:** reconstructed images or new variations.
**Architectural application:** interpolate layout or massing concepts. A smooth latent transition does not guarantee a valid design transition.
:::

:::details{summary="Diffusion · iterative denoising"}
**Idea:** learn to remove noise under a condition, then iteratively transform noise into a sample.
**Typical output:** images; other representations depend on the particular model.
**Architectural application:** explore material, atmosphere and façade concepts. Diffusion supports the image-generation experiments in this module.
:::

:::details{summary="LLM / Multimodal · language and image context"}
**Idea:** language models generate token sequences; multimodal systems can also use inputs such as images.
**Typical output:** text, captions, answers or other modalities supported by a specific model.
**Architectural application:** draft a design brief, describe a precedent, or discuss an image. A fluent answer still needs verification.
:::

:::reflect{feedback="Resemblance and adjacency constraints do not establish all architectural requirements."}
A generated floor plan resembles real plans and matches requested room adjacencies. What should you check next?
- Treat the matched adjacencies as sufficient evidence that circulation works
- *Check dimensions, circulation and structural requirements explicitly
- Use visual similarity to real plans as the main test of buildability
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
The learning objective and representation determine what a model can constrain. Visual resemblance, discrimination, or denoising is not equivalent to checking a design against explicit architectural requirements.
:::
