## One experiment from intent to evaluation

**Central question:** what can a generated possibility contribute to your design, and what still needs your architectural judgment?

Keep the process connected: **Define → Prompt → Generate → Control → Compare → Critique → Translate → Reflect**. This activity stays on one page. Your prompt, saved generations and written observations remain available when you move between its internal stages. Leaving or refreshing this page resets the activity; download your experiment record before leaving.

- **ORIGINAL** — starting output.
- **PREVIOUS** — immediately preceding successful test.
- **CURRENT** — latest successful test.

The final record compares ORIGINAL with CURRENT and retains the previous settings. Editing a control without testing does not change saved results.

:::genlab{kind=workflow}

stages: [Define, Prompt, Generate, Control, Compare, Critique, Translate, Reflect]
define: What are you trying to explore, for whom, and what would make a candidate useful?
generate: Select the initial precomputed pavilion output. Read the exact fixed prompt in Prompt; use Control to test a material change afterward.
control: Change only the material term; the other inputs in View experiment settings stay fixed. Start with Timber → Concrete. Return here to try another material; comparison uses your most recent successful pair.
expect: What do you expect the one changed variable to affect? Record this before testing.
observe: Did only the material appearance change? Compare PREVIOUS and CURRENT and name visible evidence.
unexpected: What changed unexpectedly, or stayed the same? Distinguish what you saw from what you inferred.
critique: Identify one architectural success and at least one limitation that needs checking. What evidence would resolve it?
translate: What structured information is missing before downstream evaluation? Consider geometry, dimensions, assemblies, thermal properties, schedules and HVAC.
next: What is your next architectural action, and how would you evaluate its result?
reflect: What did generation contribute, and where was your own judgment essential?
disclosure: "Mode: Precomputed model outputs — settings shown for reproducibility."
takeaway: Preserve intent, one-variable changes, comparisons, critique and missing information as one coherent design experiment.
:::

:::narration
Keep the experiment together. Define an intention, construct a condition, select an initial example and predict one change. Compare the saved states before interpreting them. Then record what the image cannot tell you and identify the information required for the next architectural step. Your final record should explain the connection between the initial design question and your judgment, rather than simply collecting attractive images.
:::

**What makes a useful experiment record?**

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
A useful record separates the design intention, exact input change, expected effect, visible evidence and unexpected consequences. It keeps the original and current prompts with the images. It names what is missing, who or what could verify it, and a concrete next step. There is no single correct image: the reasoning makes the experiment reviewable.
:::

## Reflect on your design judgment

Use your own experiment as evidence. Each question has a nearby reference response; write before revealing it.

:::reflect
What did changing one prompt variable actually control, and what changed unexpectedly?
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
Material was the only intentionally changed input; it was not an isolated geometric degree of freedom. The model may change multiple visual/design characteristics. Name what changed in your specific pair. These observed differences occurred under controlled generation, but do not establish a direct material mechanism for every difference. Prompt terms are not independent parametric design variables.
:::

:::reflect
Which qualities can you discuss visually, and which require architectural verification?
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
You can discuss apparent composition, material expression, daylight atmosphere and visible relationships. Dimensions, hidden structure, access routes and thermal performance require additional evidence. Even a visible material-like texture does not establish an assembly or its properties.
:::

:::reflect
What information is missing before simulation, and what would you do next?
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
Document units, orientation, geometry, assemblies, thermal properties, schedules and systems as appropriate to the question. Build and check a structured model, identify assumptions, and only then run a relevant simulation. Generating another image alone does not supply these inputs.
:::

:::reflect
Where was human judgment necessary, and when is generative AI most useful in your process?
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
Judgment set the brief, chose what to compare, interpreted unexpected changes and determined the next validation step. Generative AI can support early exploration and communication. Later decisions demand stronger domain integration and evidence. Consider whose aesthetics and contexts may be overrepresented in training data, and document provenance and your contribution.
:::

:::keyidea
Generate possibilities, retain meaningful control, and evaluate with architectural judgment. A useful pipeline connects imagery to evidence and accountable decisions.
:::
