## Generation Control Lab

How can we compare settings without confusing their effects?

These are **precomputed outputs from the completed course notebook**, extracted directly from its embedded PNG figures. Nothing is generated live on this website. Write a prediction, inspect the saved examples, and then unlock the next experiment.

Each group has its own architectural prompt. Compare settings **within a group**, not across tasks: Task 2 uses a community library, Task 3 a desert cultural center, Task 11 an art museum, Task 13 a civic building, and the optional resolution study a waterfront cultural center. Each panel is assigned by the notebook code and subplot order.

The cards preserve the complete saved images and their colors. Equal display boxes help comparison without imposing identical composition on the model outputs. Some panels have fewer pixels than the original generated images because the notebook saved them inside a Matplotlib figure; no detail has been regenerated or upscaled.

Same prompt, different seed: why can outputs differ?
- The prompt completely determines the output
- *The pipeline also samples from randomness

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
A prompt is a condition, not a complete image specification. Seed-dependent sampling can produce different members of the distribution. For repeatability, record the model/version, scheduler, prompt, seed and all generation settings; a seed alone is insufficient.
:::

## Interpret a controlled experiment

{{Guidance}} and a {{negative prompt}} offer steering, not guarantees. Keep a record of what you changed and what you held fixed. Compare more than one seed when investigating whether an apparent effect is consistent.

:::reflect
A high-guidance image looks closer to your prompt. Is it automatically the most useful architectural proposal?
- Yes, prompt adherence establishes architectural quality
- *No, assess both the intended qualities and architectural limitations
:::

:::details{summary="Show reference answer"}
Prompt adherence is only one criterion. A strongly emphasized façade pattern may reduce spatial coherence, and a convincing rendering still lacks verified assemblies and systems. Choose criteria that follow from the design question.
:::

:::details{summary="Why Task 4 is not the material comparison here"}
The original Task 4 changes material and seed together: timber uses seed 200, steel and glass uses 201, and rammed earth uses 202. Those outputs remain exploratory examples. The Prompt Builder and cumulative workflow now use a separate controlled rerun with timber, concrete, steel and glass, and rammed earth, all at seed 200 with matched generation settings. Only the material input term changes; multiple visual/design characteristics may still change in the outputs.
:::
